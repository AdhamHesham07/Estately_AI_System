# THE LEGAL & BUYING PROCESS FOR EGYPT REAL ESTATE

## Purchasing Primary (Off-Plan) from Developers
This is the most common route for new investors in Egypt.
1. **Expression of Interest (EOI):** An initial refundable deposit to secure a spot before a project officially launches.
2. **Contract & Downpayment:** Usually 5% to 10% of the total unit price. 
3. **Installments (Post-dated Checks):** In Egypt, developer installments are almost universally backed by post-dated bank checks. A buyer must have an Egyptian bank account and issue dozens of checks covering the 7-10 year payment plan. These checks are legally binding.
4. **Maintenance Deposit:** Typically 8% to 10% of the total unit price, paid 6 months before the delivery date. This funds the compound's perpetual upkeep.
5. **Delivery:** Properties are usually delivered 3 to 4 years from the contract date. 

## Purchasing Secondary (Resale) Properties
Buying a property from an individual who originally bought it from a developer.
1. **The "Over":** The buyer must pay the seller all the money they have paid to the developer so far, PLUS a profit margin (The "Over"). This is paid in cash upfront.
2. **Transfer of Checks:** The buyer then issues their own post-dated checks to the developer to replace the seller's original checks for the remaining installments.
3. **Transfer Fees:** Developers charge a "Transfer Fee" (usually 5% of the total unit price) to officially change the name on the contract. It is crucial to negotiate who pays this fee (Buyer or Seller) before agreeing on a final price.

## Types of Finishing
1. **Core and Shell (Red Brick):** The property is delivered as a concrete skeleton. The buyer is responsible for all internal walls, plumbing, wiring, and flooring. (Common with SODIC, Palm Hills).
2. **Semi-Finished:** Includes basic plastering and external windows, but no flooring or final fixtures.
3. **Fully Finished:** Ready to live in, complete with flooring, painted walls, and bathrooms. (Standard for Emaar, Ora, and TMG).

## Legal Registration
Most properties in new cities (New Cairo, Zayed, New Capital) are not immediately registered with the Real Estate Registry (Shahr El Akari) because the developer must finish the entire master plan first. Instead, ownership is secured through the initial developer contract and a "Tawkeel" (Power of Attorney) or a court "Seha wa Tawkea" (Signature Validity) ruling. Foreigners can easily buy property in Egypt, but there are limits on registering more than two properties officially under the state registry (though developer contracts face no limits).
